What to Wear for Women’s Rugby Training: A Starter Outfit Checklist

Rugby can look intimidating from the sidelines. Your first training session becomes simpler when you know what to ask the club and what to check in your clothes. This guide is for women exploring the sport, especially beginners joining a non-contact introduction or a club trial.

In an October 2026 interview, player Ilona Maher discussed making rugby more visible to girls and women. Her story is an invitation to try movement on your own terms; your local coach remains the best source for training and safety rules.

Before Your First Session

Ask the club whether the session is touch, non-contact, or full contact. Check the pitch surface, weather, footwear rules, and whether you need a mouthguard or other approved protection. A coach should tell you what kit is suitable; a fashion outfit guide cannot replace those instructions.

Choose Support You Can Move In

Try a sports bra during a jog, lateral shuffle, and arm reach. Notice whether the band stays put and the straps feel secure without digging in. Choose a support level that feels right for your body and the session intensity. A smooth top can make layering simple, but fit in motion matters more than the label.

Check Bottoms for Movement and Club Rules

Squat, lunge, change direction, and reach down in the bottoms you plan to wear. Check coverage, waistband movement, and whether seams rub. Many clubs specify shorts or other field kit for contact practice, so confirm their rules before arriving. Leggings can be useful for warm-ups or gym sessions when permitted.

Plan a Removable Weather Layer

Outdoor fields can feel cool while you wait and warmer once drills start. Bring a light layer that you can remove before contact or when your coach asks. Keep a dry top for the trip home. If the weather is wet, check whether any layer becomes heavy or restricts arm movement.

FAQ

Can I wear leggings to rugby training?
Ask your club. They may be useful for warm-ups or gym work, while contact sessions may specify different field kit.
Do I need specialist gear for a first session?
The answer depends on the session format. Check footwear and any protective equipment with the coach before you arrive.
What should I change into afterward?
A dry top and comfortable bottoms can make the trip home easier, especially after a wet or cold session.
