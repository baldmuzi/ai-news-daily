What to Wear for Indoor Rowing: A Women’s Sports Bra and Leggings Guide

What to Wear for Indoor Rowing: A Women’s Sports Bra and Leggings Guide

By Fanka Editor | Nov 4th, 2026 | 6 Min Read
By Fanka Editor | Nov 4th, 2026 | 6 Min Read

A good indoor rowing outfit should let you reach forward, drive through the legs, and return to the catch without needing to adjust your clothes every few strokes. You do not need a special rowing uniform. A sports bra that feels secure, leggings that stay comfortable when seated, and a light top are a sensible place to start.

A recent Women’s Fitness rowing guide explains why a machine’s damper setting is not a simple “harder workout” dial. Technique and effort matter. The same practical mindset helps with clothing: test an outfit through the actual stroke, rather than judging it only while standing still.

Test the Rowing Stroke Before You Commit

Sit on the rower with your feet secured. Reach forward with your arms, bend at the hips, and let your knees come up as you return. Then drive back and repeat at an easy pace. Notice whether your top rides up, your waistband folds uncomfortably, or a seam rubs where you sit.

A fitting-room squat can reveal some issues, but the repeated seated position is a better check for rowing. If a garment distracts you after a minute, try a different fit before increasing the pace.

Choose Sports Bra Support for Your Session

Indoor rowing usually involves less vertical bounce than running, but the repeated reach and pull still make bra fit important. Check the band as you lean forward and as you finish the stroke. It should stay in place without digging into your ribs. Straps should let your shoulders move naturally.

Support needs vary with body shape, breast size, pace, and personal comfort. A light-support style may suit some people; others will prefer more coverage or structure. Use Fanka’s current product guidance and size chart, then test the motion yourself.

Check the Leggings Waistband While Seated

A waistband that feels fine when standing can feel different at the catch. Bend forward, sit tall, and repeat a few strokes. Look for a secure fit that does not roll, pinch, or require constant pulling up. Check the inner-thigh seams and the fabric at the seat, too.

Close-fitting leggings can keep loose fabric away from moving parts, but compression is a personal preference. Choose a level of hold that feels comfortable during the full range of motion. If a pocket carries a phone, remove it for the rowing session so it does not press against your hip.

Plan Fabric and Layers Around the Room

Studios and gyms can feel cool when you arrive and warmer after a few minutes. A breathable top with a removable light layer gives you options. Pay attention to how the fabric feels as you sweat and whether seams or straps shift with each pull.

For a commute home, bring a dry layer if you expect a temperature change. Check any fabric or care claim on the actual product page rather than assuming all activewear performs the same way.

FAQ

Do I need compression leggings for rowing?
No. Some women like a close fit, but comfort through the seated stroke matters more than a garment label.
Can I wear a light-support bra?
That depends on your body, pace, and preference. Test the band and straps while rowing at the effort you plan to use.
What should I avoid wearing?
Avoid anything that catches on the machine, rubs as you move, or needs frequent adjustment. Check the equipment instructions at your gym.
