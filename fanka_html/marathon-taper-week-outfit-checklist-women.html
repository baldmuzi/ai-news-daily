What to Wear During Marathon Taper Week: A Women’s Comfort and Packing Guide

The week before a marathon can feel full of decisions. Your outfit can be one of the simple ones: choose pieces you have already worn in training, check the forecast, and pack a small backup for changing conditions. A new race-week purchase is rarely worth an untested fit.

American runner Weini Kelati described her gradual move toward a first marathon in a recent interview. Her professional training is individual; this guide focuses on practical clothing decisions any runner can review with her own coach and race information.

Keep Your Race Outfit Familiar

Choose a sports bra, top, bottoms, socks, and shoes you have used for a long run. Think through where seams sat, whether a waistband moved, and whether pockets were easy to reach. You do not need a perfect-looking matching set if a familiar combination feels better.

Plan for the Start, the Run, and the Finish

Race mornings can feel cooler while you wait and warmer after you begin moving. Check the official race forecast close to the event, then choose a removable outer layer if needed. Bring a dry layer for after the finish. Consider your own comfort with rain, wind, and sweat rather than following a fixed temperature rule.

Do a Short Movement Check

During an easy pre-race outing, notice whether your bra band stays comfortable, your top rides up, your leggings or shorts rub, and your phone or fuel bounces in a pocket. If something bothers you, return to a combination that has already worked. Avoid using this short check to test a brand-new compression level.

A Note from Fanka

The most useful race-week outfit is one that lets you focus on the experience. Fit and comfort are personal, so use your own training notes. Fanka products are not medical-grade and medical concerns should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Make Race Morning Easier

Set out your tested outfit, bib, and layers the night before. Keep any nutrition plan with your coach or clinician’s advice. After the race, a dry change of clothes can make the trip home more comfortable. Your clothes should help you move simply, not add one more decision at the start line.

FAQ

Should I wear new leggings on race day?
A long run in the same pair gives you much better information about fit and rubbing than a quick try-on.
Are compression leggings required for a marathon?
No. Choose the fit and coverage you prefer, and test any close-fitting garment in training.
What if the weather changes?
Review the event forecast and rules, then pack a familiar alternate layer or bottom if conditions may change.
